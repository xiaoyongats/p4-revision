Golden rules

1. Answer is ONE sentence→2. Use the given word exactly where it is placed→3. Same meaning, same tense→4. Do not repeat the name: use he/she/it→5. Capital letter + full stop

If the word is printed at the start of the line, your sentence starts with it. If printed at the end of a blank, it goes in the middle.

2

Before, after, when, while

Siti washes the plates. Then she places them on the table. (before)
before + -ing(no need for "she" again)
Siti washes the plates before placing them on the table.
Mrs Tan saw a rat in the drain. Mrs Tan screamed. (When …)
Whenfirst action, comma, second action
When Mrs Tan saw a rat in the drain, she screamed.
Peter was listening to music. Peter's sister was talking on the phone. (while)
whiletwo actions at the same time
Peter was listening to music while his sister was talking on the phone.
He finished his homework. He watched TV. (After …)
After + -ingor After he finished …
After finishing his homework, he watched TV.
3

Which and who

who for people · which for things and animals. Put it right after the noun it describes.

The flowers are pretty. The flowers are in the vase. (which)
noun + whichThe flowers which are in the vase …
The flowers which are in the vase are pretty.
The boy is my cousin. He won the race. (who)The boy who won the race is my cousin.
4

Although (surprise)

Sarah was tired. Sarah stayed up late to finish her work. (Although …)
Although…, she … (never add "but")
Although Sarah was tired, she stayed up late to finish her work.

Despite / In spite of + -ing or noun: Despite being tired, Sarah stayed up late.

5

Either … or · Neither … nor · Both … and

We can play soccer during recess. We can go to the library during recess. (either)We can either play soccer or go to the library during recess.
Tom does not like durian. Mei does not like durian. (Neither …)
norverb matches the nearer subject; drop "not"
Neither Tom nor Mei likes durian.
Ali can sing. Ali can dance. (both)Ali can both sing and dance.
She is clever. She is kind. (not only)She is not only clever but also kind.
6

If and unless

The students arrive on time. We will start the lesson. (If …)If the students arrive on time, we will start the lesson.
You must hurry. If not, you will miss the bus. (Unless …)
unless= if … not
Unless you hurry, you will miss the bus.
7

Reported speech

"I'm going to the park tomorrow," Ravi told his brother.

Ravi told his brother that he was going to the park the next day.

ChangeSpokenReported
PersonI / my / wehe, she / his, her / they
Tense: one step backam / is · go · will · canwas · went · would · could
Timetoday · tomorrow · yesterday · nowthat day · the next day / the following day · the day before · then
Placehere · thisthere · that

Questions: "Where do you live?" Mei asked me. → Mei asked me where I lived. (no question mark)

8

Watch out!

✗Although Sarah was tired, but she stayed up late.
✓Never use although + but together.
✗Siti washes the plates before she placing them.
✓before placing them, or before she places them.
✗Ravi told his brother that he is going to the park tomorrow.
✓was going … the next day.
✗Writing two sentences.
✓Only one full stop.

Remember in 3 lines

One sentence, the given word, the same meaning and tense.
before/after + -ing · which/who right after the noun · although never with but.
Reported speech: I → he/she, one tense back, tomorrow → the next day.
