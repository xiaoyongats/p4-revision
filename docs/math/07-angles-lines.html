Kinds of angles

A straight line is 180°. A right angle is marked with a small square. Name angles as ∠x or ∠ABC (the middle letter is the corner).

2

Measuring with a protractor

1. Estimate: acute or obtuse?→2. Centre on the corner→3. Base line on 0°→4. Read the scale that starts at 0

If the angle looks acute your answer must be below 90°. If you read 135° for an acute angle, you used the wrong scale → it is 45°.

Short arms? Extend the lines with a ruler before measuring.

3

Finding unknown angles

Every corner of a square or rectangle is 90°. Split parts add up to 90°.

A right angle is split into 48° and ∠y.
∠y90° − 48°
42°
Square corner split into 18°, ∠x and 22°.
∠x90° − 18° − 22°
50°
Rectangle corner split into ∠x, 36° and ∠y. ∠x is twice ∠y.
x + y90° − 36° = 54°3 units54° → 1 unit = 18°
∠y = 18°, ∠x = 36°
Two corners at C: rectangle (∠DCB = 90°) and square (∠ECG = 90°). ∠DCE = 67°, ∠BCF = 35°. Find ∠FCG.
∠ECB90° − 67° = 23°∠FCG90° − 23° − 35°

4

Squares and rectangles

Rectangle

4 right angles · opposite sides equal and parallel

Square

4 right angles · all 4 sides equal · opposite sides parallel

Completing a square on a grid

If side PQ goes 3 right and 2 up, the next side turns 90°: 2 left and 3 up. Count squares; do not guess with a ruler.

5

Parallel and perpendicular lines

Parallel ( ∥ )

Never meet. Always the same distance apart. On a grid: same "steps" (e.g. 2 right, 1 up).

Perpendicular ( ⊥ )

Meet at a right angle. A vertical line is perpendicular to a horizontal line.

6

Watch out!

✗Reading the outer scale for an acute angle → 135°
✓Estimate first. Acute must be less than 90°.
✗Forgetting a part when subtracting from 90°.
✓List every part of the corner: all parts add to 90°.
✗Drawing a "square" that only looks square.
✓Count grid steps for each side.

Remember in 3 lines

Acute < 90° · right = 90° · obtuse between 90° and 180°.
Corners of squares and rectangles = 90°. Parts add up to 90°.
Parallel never meet. Perpendicular meet at 90°.
