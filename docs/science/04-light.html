Light

1

Sources of light

A source of light gives off its own light. Light is a form of energy; it is not matter.

Sources ✓

  • Sun and stars
  • fire, candle flame
  • lit lamp, torch, bulb
  • firefly, lightning

NOT sources ✗

  • the Moon (reflects the Sun's light)
  • mirror
  • eyes
  • shiny things, reflective strips

A torch or lamp is only a source when it is switched on.

2

How we see things

  • We see an object when light from a source falls on it, is reflected off it, and enters our eyes.
  • We see a light source when its light goes straight into our eyes.
  • Arrows always go from the light source and into the eye. Light never comes out of the eye.
  • In a completely dark room we see nothing, not even a mirror. There is no light to reflect.
3

Light travels in straight lines

Straight tube

We can see a flame through it. Light goes straight through and enters the eye.

Bent tube

We cannot see the flame. Light cannot go round the bend.

Other proof

Torch beams are straight. Shadows form because light cannot bend around objects.

4

Reflection

Reflect MORE light

Smooth, shiny, light-coloured: mirror, aluminium foil, white paper.

Reflect LESS light

Dark, dull, rough: black cloth, tree bark.

  • Reflective strips on vests and helmets help drivers see cyclists at night. Choose the material that can be seen from the furthest distance.
  • To measure light accurately, use a light sensor connected to a datalogger, not your eyes.

How much light passes through?

Opaque
TransparentMost light passes through. We can see clearly through it.
clear glass, clear plastic, clean water, air
TranslucentSome light passes through. We cannot see clearly through it.
frosted glass, tissue paper, tracing paper, cooking oil
OpaqueNo light passes through.
wood, metal, cardboard, rubber, brick

Uses

Window: transparent. Bathroom window: frosted glass lets light in but gives privacy. Curtains: opaque.

Sunglasses

Choose a material that blocks some light (protects the eyes) but lets some light through (so you can see).

Light-sensor experiments

  • More light detected = the material lets more light through.
  • Keep the same: torch, distance, type of light sensor, size and thickness of the object, type of container.
  • Never tick the result (amount of light measured) as a variable to keep the same.
6

Watch out!

✗The Moon is a source of light.
✓The Moon reflects light from the Sun.
✗Light goes from the eye to the object.
✓Light goes from the source → object → into the eye.
✗Translucent materials let all light through.
✓Translucent lets some light through.
✗A mirror can be seen in a totally dark room.
✓No light means nothing to reflect, so we see nothing.

Remember in 3 lines

Source gives off its own light. Moon and mirror do NOT.
Light source → object → reflected → eye
Transparent: most · Translucent: some · Opaque: none
