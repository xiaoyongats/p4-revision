Effects of Heat

1

Who gains heat? Who loses heat?

The hotter thing loses heat. Everything colder that it touches gains heat.

Ice cream out of the freezerThe ice cream gains heat from the surrounding air and melts.
Eating ice creamIce cream gains heat. The cup and your mouth lose heat.
Spoon in hot coffeeThe spoon gains heat from the coffee.
Glass of ice in a basin of hot waterHot water loses heat. The basin, glass and ice gain heat.
Holding iceYour hand loses heat to the ice, so it feels cold.
2

Sources of heat

Sources ✓

  • the Sun (main source)
  • fire, candle flame
  • lit lamp, iron, oven
  • friction (rubbing hands)
  • our bodies

NOT sources ✗

  • woollen jacket (it only slows down heat loss)
  • ice
  • a mirror

In an experiment, the boiling water or the burner is the source of heat.

3

Good and poor conductors of heat

Good conductors

Heat flows through quickly.
Metals: copper, iron, steel, aluminium (and foil).

Poor conductors

Heat flows through slowly.
wood, plastic, rubber, glass, cloth, wool, styrofoam, air.

Pot / panMetal: heat flows quickly to the food.
HandlePlastic or wood: heat flows slowly to the hand.
Cooler box / lunch boxStyrofoam: keeps cold drinks cold and hot food hot.
Wool, feathersTrap air. Air is a poor conductor, so less heat is lost from the body.

Wax on rods experiment

Rod A: 20 s
best
Rod C: 35 s
Rod B: 45 s
poorest
  • Shortest time for the wax to melt → best conductor.
  • Longest time → poorest conductor → best for a handle.
  • Wax pieces along one rod: the one that drops first is closest to the heat source.
  • Keep the same: rod length and thickness, amount of wax, distance from the hot water. (The material is what you change.)

Wrapped flasks: cooling graph

The line that drops fastest → best conductor (aluminium foil). The line that drops slowest → poorest conductor (cloth) → best to keep food hot and drinks cold.

Metal feels colder than wood

They are at the same temperature. Metal conducts heat away from your hand faster, so it feels colder.

4

Expansion and contraction

gains heat→expands    loses heat→contracts

Mass stays the same. Only the volume changes. Gases expand the most.

Dented ping-pong ballIn hot water, the air inside expands and pushes the dent out.
Stopper pops outNear a fire, the air in the bottle gains heat and expands.
Tight metal lidRun hot water over it. The metal lid expands more than the glass and loosens.
Gaps between tiles / railsRoom to expand. On a hot day the tiles expand, so the gaps get narrower.
Cables sagOn hot days the cables expand and get longer.
Two stuck glassesIce in the inner glass: it contracts. Hot water around the outer glass: it expands. They come apart.

3-step answer

What gains or loses heat?→Expands or contracts→What happens
5

Changes of state

Heating ice: the temperature graph

While melting or boiling, the temperature stays the same until the change is complete.

Boiling

At 100 °C, fast, bubbles all through the liquid.

Evaporation

At any temperature, slow, only at the surface.

Faster evaporation (drying clothes)

Higher temperature (sunny day)
Wind (fan, windy place)
Larger exposed surface area (spread out)
Drier air

Sweat evaporates by taking heat from our skin, so we feel cooler.

Other things change state too: liquid wax loses heat and freezes into solid wax; ice on frozen food gains heat and melts.

6

Where do water droplets come from?

Water vapour in the warmer air→ touches a cooler surface→ loses heat, condenses→ water droplets

Use these 3 steps for 3 marks.

Glass of ice waterDroplets on the outside.
Air-con car windowDroplets on the outside (warm air is outside).
Mirror after a hot showerDroplets on the room side (warm air is inside).

Water vapour is invisible. The white "steam" above a kettle is tiny water droplets.

7

Watch out!

✗A woollen jacket gives off heat.
✓It slows down heat loss. It is not a source.
✗The gaps between tiles expand on a hot day.
✓The tiles expand, so the gaps get narrower.
✗Longest time for wax to melt = best conductor.
✓Shortest time = best conductor.
✗Use aluminium foil to keep food hot.
✓Foil is a good conductor. Use a poor conductor such as cloth.
✗Stuck glasses: put hot water inside.
✓Inner glass cold, outer glass hot.
✗Droplets on a cold glass came through the glass.
✓They came from water vapour in the air.
✗The temperature keeps rising while ice melts.
✓It stays at 0 °C until all the ice has melted.

Remember in 3 lines

Metals are good conductors. Plastic, wood, cloth, air are poor conductors.
Gain heat → expand. Lose heat → contract. Mass stays the same.
Melting / boiling / evaporation gain heat. Freezing / condensation lose heat.
