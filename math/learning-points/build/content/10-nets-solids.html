1

The solids

Cube6 equal square faces
Cuboid6 rectangular faces (opposite faces are the same)
Prism2 identical end faces joined by rectangles. Triangular prism: 2 triangles + 3 rectangles. Same shape all the way along.
Pyramid1 base + triangles that meet at one point (the apex). Square pyramid: 1 square + 4 triangles.
Cone, cylinderHave a curved surface. Not prisms or pyramids.

Prism or pyramid? Pointed top → pyramid. Same cross-section all along, two matching ends → prism.

2

Nets of a cube

A cube net has 6 squares. There are 11 different cube nets.

Signs it IS a net

A row of 4 with one square above and one below (anywhere along the row) · staircase shapes · 3 + 3 rows shifted

Signs it is NOT

A 2 × 2 block · a row of 5 or 6 · two squares on the same side of a row of 4, so they overlap when folded

Test it: pick one square as the bottom and fold the others up in your head. Two faces landing on the same place → not a net.

3

Nets of a triangular prism

3 rectangles in a row + 2 triangles. The triangles must sit on the long edges of the strip (one above, one below) so they close the two open ends of the tube.

If both triangles are stuck on the short ends of the strip, they meet the edge that joins up to close the tube → not a net.

5

Watch out!

✗Any 6 squares joined together make a cube net.
✓No 2 × 2 block, no row longer than 4, no overlaps.
✗A square pyramid is a prism.
✓Triangles meeting at a point → pyramid.
✗Triangles on the short ends of a prism net.
✓Triangles go on the long edges, one on each side.

Remember in 3 lines

Prism: 2 matching ends + rectangles. Pyramid: base + triangles to a point.
Cube net: 6 squares, no 2 × 2 block, row of at most 4.
Cube: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices.
