## Key learning points

### Common materials
**Wood, metal, plastic, glass, rubber, fabric (cloth), ceramic, paper.**

### Properties

| Property | Meaning | Good examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | Can hold a heavy load **without breaking** | steel, iron, wood |
| **Hardness** | Cannot be **scratched** easily | metal, glass, diamond |
| **Flexibility** | Can **bend without breaking** | rubber, cloth, plastic sheet, thin wire |
| **Waterproof** | Does **not let water pass through** | plastic, rubber, glass, metal |
| **Absorbency** | Can **soak up** water | cloth (towel), sponge, tissue, paper |
| **Ability to float** | Floats on water | wood, cork, styrofoam, some plastics |
| **Transparency** | Lets light pass through (see Light chapter) | clear glass, clear plastic |
| **Heat conduction** | Lets heat pass through quickly (see Heat chapter) | metals |

### Matching material to use
| Object | Needed properties | Material |
|---|---|---|
| Window pane | transparent | glass (disadvantage: breaks easily) |
| Umbrella canopy | **flexible + waterproof** | treated cloth / plastic |
| Shirt | **flexible + absorbent** (NOT waterproof, so sweat is absorbed) | cloth |
| Towel | **absorbent** | cloth |
| Nail, hammer head | **strong, hard, not flexible** | steel |
| Bookshelf / chair | **strong** | wood / metal |
| Raincoat | waterproof, flexible | plastic / rubber |
| Life jacket | floats | styrofoam / cork |

### Method: material-test experiments
1. Find **what is measured** (number of weights before breaking → strength; distance the strip bends down → flexibility; amount of water absorbed → absorbency).
2. Translate it: **more weights held = stronger**; **bends down more = more flexible**.
3. Choose the material whose property suits the use and **quote the data**.
4. "Why repeat 3 times?" → to make the results **more reliable**.
5. Changed variable = **type of material**.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Fill in with words from: heat, breaks, metal, bends, light, glass.
"Part A of a window is made of ____ because it allows ____ to pass through so that we can see the trees outside. However, part A ____ easily when dropped." [3]
**A:** glass; light; breaks

**Q2 (MCQ).** Which object is NOT made of a waterproof material?
(1) sheet of glass (2) umbrella (3) towel (4) plastic fork
**A:** (3)

**Q3 (MCQ).** Properties: A waterproof; B waterproof + floats; C flexible; D flexible + floats. Best material for a shirt?
**A:** **C**: flexible and not waterproof (so it can absorb sweat).

**Q4.** Masses were added to a strip of material across two tables. Distance from strip to floor (start 10 cm): material A 10 → 8 → 6 → 4 cm; material B 10 → 10 → 9 → 9 cm (0, 10, 20, 30 g).
(a) Name the property tested and say what it means. [2] (b) How does the mass affect the distance? [1] (c) Which is better for an umbrella's canopy? Why? [1]
**A:** (a) **Flexibility**: the ability to **bend without breaking**. (b) **The more mass added, the shorter the distance** (the strip bends more). (c) **A.** The distance decreased more, so A is **more flexible**.

**Q5 (MCQ).** Steel is used to make nails because it
(1) is shiny (2) is strong (3) is magnetic (4) sinks in water
**A:** (2)

**Q6 (MCQ).** A hammer head (part W) should be:
(1) not strong, not flexible (2) not strong, flexible (3) strong, flexible (4) strong, not flexible
**A:** (4)

**Q7.** Chairs held this many 5 kg weights before breaking (3 trials): A 4, 5, 3; B 7, 9, 7; C 2, 1, 1.
(a) Which material is best for a bookshelf? Explain. [2] (b) Changed variable? [1] (c) Why 3 trials? [1]
**A:** (a) **B.** It held the **most weights** before breaking (7–9), so it is the **strongest** and can hold the most books. (b) **Type of material.** (c) To make the results **more reliable**.

**Q8.** Why is a raincoat made of plastic and not cloth? [1]
**A:** Plastic is **waterproof**, so it does not let water through.

**Q9.** Suggest why a cooking pot handle is made of plastic and not metal. [1]
**A:** Plastic is a **poor conductor of heat**, so heat flows slowly to the hand.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing up **strong** (doesn't break under load) and **hard** (doesn't scratch).
- Mixing up **flexible** (bends) with **elastic/stretchy**.
- In the bending test, choosing the strip that bends **least** as the most flexible.
- Forgetting to give the **disadvantage** when asked (glass **breaks** easily).
