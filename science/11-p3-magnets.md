## Key learning points

### Magnetic and non-magnetic materials
| Magnetic (attracted) | Non-magnetic (not attracted) |
|---|---|
| **iron, steel, nickel, cobalt** | wood, plastic, glass, rubber, paper, fabric, **aluminium, copper, brass, gold, silver** |

⚠️ **Not all metals are magnetic.** Aluminium cans and copper wire are **not** attracted.

### Properties of magnets
1. Attract **magnetic materials**.
2. Have **two poles**: **North (N)** and **South (S)**.
3. **Strongest at the poles** (most paper clips cling to the **ends**).
4. **Like poles repel** (N–N, S–S); **unlike poles attract** (N–S).
5. A **freely suspended** magnet (hanging, or floating on styrofoam in water) comes to rest pointing **North–South**.
6. Magnetic force can **act through** non-magnetic materials (paper, plastic, glass, water) and **without touching**.

### ⭐ Attraction vs repulsion
- **Attracted** to a magnet → it could be a **magnetic material OR a magnet** (unlike pole facing).
- **Repelled** → it **must be a magnet**. *Repulsion is the only sure test.*
- **No interaction** → **non-magnetic** material.
- Too far from the magnet (e.g. 15 cm while the others were tested at 5 cm) → **not possible to tell**.

### Compass
- A compass needle is a **tiny magnet**; its N end points to the Earth's north.
- Near a magnet: the needle's **N end points towards the magnet's S pole** (and away from its N pole).

### Making magnets
| Method | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stroke method** | Stroke an iron/steel bar with **one pole** of a magnet, **in one direction**, many times, lifting the magnet away at the end | The end **where the stroke finishes** becomes the **opposite** pole to the stroking pole. Stroking with **N** from X to Y → **Y = S**, X = N |
| **Electrical method** | Coil insulated wire around an iron/steel bar and connect to a **battery** | Makes an **electromagnet** |

- **Iron** → **temporary** magnet (loses magnetism easily). **Steel** → **permanent** magnet.
- Magnets lose magnetism if **dropped, hammered or heated**.

### Electromagnets
- A coil of wire around an **iron core**, connected to a **battery**. It is a magnet **only while the current flows**.
- **Battery removed** → no current → **loses its magnetism** → attracts nothing.
- **Stronger** with: **more batteries**, **more coils (turns) of wire**. Weaker with fewer batteries or fewer coils.
- The core must be a **magnetic material** (iron); a wooden core does not work.
- It can only attract **magnetic** materials (paper clips yes; wooden toothpick no).
- Uses: **cranes in scrapyards** (switch off to drop the metal), electric bells, maglev trains.

### Uses of magnets
Fridge door seals, compasses, magnetic toys, **sorting steel cans from aluminium cans**, **maglev trains** (repulsion lifts the train).

### Method: testing magnet strength
- **More** paper clips attracted, or attracted from a **greater distance** → **stronger** magnet.
- Keep constant: type of clips/nail, distance, same surface.

### Method: floating / maglev questions
- For the train to **float**, the faces of the two bars that face each other must be **like poles**, so they **repel**.
- For bars placed one above the other (AB on top of CD): **A over C must be the same pole, and B over D the same pole**. e.g. N–S over N–S ✅; S–N over S–N ✅; N–S over S–N ❌ (attract).

## Practice questions

**Q1 (MCQ).** A magnet is brought near a plastic block. The block will
(1) move up (2) not move (3) move left (4) move right
**A:** (2). Plastic is non-magnetic.

**Q2 (MCQ).** Which can be attracted by a magnet? iron rod / glass rod / plastic rod / wooden rod
**A:** **Iron rod**

**Q3 (MCQ).** In which arrangement do two magnets push each other away?
**A:** The one where **like poles face each other** (e.g. S facing S, or N facing N).

**Q4 (MCQ).** Hanging object A swings **towards** magnet X; hanging object B swings **away**. Which is correct?
**A:** A: **magnetic material** (it could also be a magnet, but the best option is magnetic material); B: **magnet**.

**Q5 (MCQ).** Rods near a magnet: P attracted, Q repelled, R no interaction, S attracted. Which conclusions can be made? A Q is a magnet B S is wood C R is magnetic D P, Q, S are magnetic materials.
**A:** **A and D.** (S is attracted, so it is not wood; R had no interaction, so it is non-magnetic.)

**Q6 (MCQ).** A magnet is held 5 cm above W (attracted), 5 cm above Y (not attracted) and 15 cm above Z (not attracted). Which is correct?
**A:** W **magnetic**; Y **non-magnetic**; Z **not possible to tell** (it was tested from a greater distance, which is not a fair test).

**Q7 (MCQ).** A bar magnet is taped to a styrofoam board floating freely in water. How does it come to rest?
**A:** Its **N pole points North** and its S pole points South.

**Q8.** Ring magnet B floats above ring magnet A on a glass holder.
(a) The holder did not attract the magnets. "Glass is a ______ material." (b) Why does B float above A? [2]
**A:** (a) **non-magnetic**. (b) B is **repelling** A: **like poles are facing** each other.

**Q9 (MCQ).** Bars AB (on the train) and CD (on the base) make a model train float. Which pole arrangements work? P: N–S over N–S. Q: N–S over S–N. R: S–N over N–S. S: S–N over S–N.
**A:** **P and S only**: like poles face each other, so they repel.

**Q10.** An iron nail is stroked from V to W with the N pole of a magnet.
(a) Name the method. (b) Which pole is W? (c) Iron bar L is stroked from X to Y with the N pole, then Y is brought near the N pole of a hanging magnet Z. What happens? Explain. [2]
**A:** (a) **Stroke method**. (b) **South** (the end where the stroke finishes is opposite to the stroking pole). (c) They **attract**. Y becomes a **S pole**, and **unlike poles attract**.

**Q11.** A magnet lifted from a tray of paper clips has most clips at its two ends. Conclusion? [1]
**A:** The magnet is **strongest at its poles**.

**Q12.** Set-up A: an iron nail with **few coils** of wire attracted 3 steel pins. Set-up B: same nail and batteries with **more coils** attracted 5 pins.
(a) Aim? (b) How to attract more pins with B without changing the batteries or coils? (c) To test the number of batteries with A, state two changes. (d) The nail is removed from B. Why can't it attract pins?
**A:** (a) To find out if the **number of coils of wire** around the iron nail affects the **number of steel pins attracted**. (b) Use **smaller / lighter steel pins** (or a bigger iron core). (c) **Change the number of batteries**; make the **number of coils the same** as B. (d) There is **no battery/current**, so the nail is **no longer an electromagnet**. (Iron is a temporary magnet.)

**Q13 (MCQ).** To test "number of batteries affects electromagnet strength", which two set-ups? A: 2 batteries, 20 coils. B: 3 batteries, 20 coils. C: 2 batteries, 10 coils. D: 2 batteries, 10 coils, no iron core.
**A:** **A and B**

**Q14 (MCQ).** To test "number of coils": A 2 batteries/10 coils; B 2/5; C 3/10; D 3/5. Which two?
**A:** **A and B** (or C and D). Same batteries, different coils.

**Q15 (MCQ).** Which action would **reduce** the number of steel clips an electromagnet attracts?
(1) move it nearer the tray (2) add more clips to the tray (3) add another battery (4) coil fewer turns of wire
**A:** (4)

**Q16.** An electromagnet (battery + coil around an iron rod) is placed near paper clips.
(a) What is observed? (b) Explain. (c) Clips replaced by a wooden toothpick? (d) Battery removed?
**A:** (a) The iron rod **attracts the paper clips**. (b) The iron rod has become an **electromagnet**. (c) The toothpick is **not attracted** (wood is non-magnetic). (d) The clips **fall / are not attracted**: no current, so the rod **loses its magnetism**.

**Q17 (MCQ).** Magnets X, Y, Z attracted an iron nail from 10, 5 and 15 cm. Z is the largest and Y the smallest. Conclusion?
**A:** **The bigger the magnet, the stronger it is** (for these magnets). Z is the strongest because it attracted the nail from the greatest distance.

**Q18.** A compass needle's N end points towards end P of a magnet. What pole is P? [1]
**A:** **South** (unlike poles attract).

## Common mistakes
- Saying **all metals** are magnetic (aluminium and copper are not).
- Saying "attracted, so it must be a magnet". **Only repulsion proves a magnet.**
- Choosing set-ups where **two** variables change.
- Saying an electromagnet works without a battery.
- Stroking method: mixing up which end becomes which pole. **Finishing end = opposite pole** to the stroking pole.
