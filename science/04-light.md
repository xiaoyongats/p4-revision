# Chapter 4: Light

## Seen in real papers
- "Which is a source of light?" → **star in the sky**, **candle flame**, fire, lamp. NOT the moon, mirror, eyes, flower, pear (ACS, CHS, Henry Park).
- Arrow diagrams: how Sue sees a book (lamp → book → eye) (Henry Park, CHS).
- Hollow tube: we see a flame because **light travels in a straight line** and **light from the flame enters the eye** (ACS).
- **Frosted glass jar** over a torch makes the room dimmer: it **blocks some light** (ACS).
- **Light sensor + datalogger** experiments: water / milk / cooking oil; objects A, B, C made of different materials (ACS, CHS).
- Which material reflects light best → use for a **cyclist's helmet** (CHS).
- Sunglasses lens: choose the material that **lets some light through** (Henry Park).

## 4.1 Sources of light

- **Light is a form of energy.** It is **not matter**.
- A **source of light** gives off its **own** light.

| ✅ Sources of light | ❌ NOT sources (they only **reflect** light) |
|---|---|
| Sun, **stars**, fire, **candle flame**, lit torch/lamp/bulb, firefly, lightning, TV screen (switched on) | **Moon**, mirror, eyes, glittery/shiny objects, cat's eyes, reflective strips, planets |

- The **moon is not a source**: it **reflects light from the Sun**.
- A torch or lamp is only a source when it is **switched on / lit**.

## 4.2 How we see things

- We see an object when **light from a light source** falls on the object, is **reflected** off the object, and **enters our eyes**.
- We see a **light source** when its light travels **directly into our eyes**.
- **Arrows always point AWAY from the light source and INTO the eye.** Light never comes out of the eyes.
- In a **completely dark** room we cannot see anything, not even a white object or a mirror, because there is no light to be reflected.
- **Light travels in straight lines.** Evidence:
  - We can see a flame through a **straight** hollow tube but not through a **bent** one.
  - Shadows are formed (Chapter 5).
  - Beams of light from a torch or through gaps in clouds are straight.

## Reflection
- **All** objects we see reflect some light.
- **Smooth, shiny, light-coloured** surfaces (mirror, aluminium foil, white paper) reflect **more** light. **Dark, dull, rough** surfaces reflect **less**.
- Uses: mirrors (car side mirrors, convex mirrors at road bends), **reflective strips** on vests, helmets, road signs and bicycles help others **see us at night**.

## Transparency: how much light passes through

| Type | Light passing through | Examples | Shadow (Ch 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** | **Most** light passes through; we can see clearly through it | clear glass, clear plastic, clean water, air | no / very faint shadow |
| **Translucent** | **Some** light passes through; we cannot see clearly through it | **frosted glass**, tracing paper, **tissue paper**, wax paper, cooking oil | **light (faint)** shadow |
| **Opaque** | **No** light passes through | wood, metal, cardboard, rubber, brick, book | **dark** shadow |

- Uses: windows (transparent, so we can see out and light comes in); bathroom windows of **frosted glass** (light comes in, but **privacy**); **opaque** curtains to block light; **sunglasses** that are **translucent/tinted**, letting **some** light through so we can still see without hurting our eyes.
- **Measure light accurately** with a **light sensor connected to a datalogger** (not with our eyes).

### Method: light-sensor experiments
1. **More light detected** = material lets **more light pass through** (or reflects more light, if the sensor measures reflection).
2. Order the materials by their readings.
3. Keep constant: **type and amount of light from the torch**, **distance** between torch, object and sensor, **type of light sensor**, **size/thickness** of the object or **amount** of liquid, **type of container**. Do **not** tick the result being measured.

## Practice questions

**Q1 (MCQ).** Which is a source of light?
(1) a flower (2) a star in the sky (3) the moon (4) an apple
**A:** (2)

**Q2 (MCQ).** Which is a source of light?
(1) a mirror (2) a candle flame (3) a pear (4) the moon
**A:** (2)

**Q3.** Tick the sources of light: mirror, eyes, fire, lamp. [2]
**A:** **fire** and **lamp** only.

**Q4.** Explain why the moon is not a source of light. [1]
**A:** It does not give off its own light; it **reflects light from the Sun**.

**Q5 (MCQ).** Which diagram explains how Sue sees a book under a lamp?
**A:** The one with arrows **from the lamp to the book**, then **from the book to Sue's eye**. Reject any diagram with an arrow from the eye, or with no arrow reaching the eye.

**Q6.** Fill in: "The ______ from the lamp is ______ by the ball and enters Lucian's eye." [2]
**A:** **light**; **reflected**

**Q7 (MCQ).** Anna sees a flame through a straight hollow tube. Which two statements explain this? A Light travels in a straight line. B Light from the flame enters her eyes. C Light is reflected away from her eyes. D Light from her eyes is reflected off the flame.
**A:** **A and B**

**Q8.** Anna bends the tube. Can she still see the flame? Explain. [1]
**A:** **No.** Light **travels in straight lines** and cannot travel around the bend to reach her eyes.

**Q9 (MCQ).** A frosted glass jar is placed over a lit torch and the room becomes dimmer. Why?
(1) Light is reflected into Ally's eyes. (2) No light is reflected from the torch. (3) The jar blocks all light. (4) The jar blocks some light.
**A:** (4). Frosted glass is **translucent**.

**Q10.** Light sensors under beakers of water, milk and cooking oil (same amount) measured light from identical torches: water 300 units, oil 150 units, milk ___.
(a) Suggest a likely reading for milk. (b) State a property of light shown. (c) Name two variables to keep constant. (d) What could be added to the water so that less light is detected?
**A:** (a) Less than 150, e.g. **100** units (milk lets the least light through). (b) **Light can pass through some materials**; different materials allow different amounts of light to pass through. (c) **Type of beaker**, **type of light sensor**, **amount of light given off by the torch**, **amount of liquid**. (d) Add some **milk / cooking oil** and stir it in.

**Q11 (MCQ).** Objects A, B, C (same size and thickness, different materials) gave light readings 400, 150 and 0. What was the aim?
**A:** To find out how the **material** of an object affects the **amount of light that passes through** it.

**Q12.** In Q11, classify A, B and C as transparent, translucent or opaque. [1]
**A:** A: transparent; B: translucent; C: opaque.

**Q13.** Lucian shone a torch at materials X, Y, Z on a wall and walked towards each until he could see it clearly. Distance at which he saw it: X 300 cm, Y 330 cm, Z 360 cm.
(a) Which material is best for a cyclist's night helmet? Why? (b) Name equipment to measure reflected light more accurately.
**A:** (a) **Z.** It could be seen clearly from the **furthest distance**, so it **reflects the most light**. (b) **Light sensor with a datalogger.**

**Q14.** Sunglasses lens: material X lets most light through, Y lets no light through, Z lets some light through. Which is best? Explain. [2]
**A:** **Z.** It **blocks some light**, protecting the eyes, but **allows some light through**, so he can still see.

**Q15.** Why can't we see anything in a completely dark room, even a mirror? [1]
**A:** There is **no light** to be **reflected** off the objects into our eyes.

## Common mistakes
- Saying the **moon** or a **mirror** is a light source.
- Drawing arrows **from the eye** to the object.
- Saying "we see because our eyes shine on objects".
- Saying translucent materials "let all light through". They let **some** light through.
- Ticking the **measured result** (amount of light detected) as a variable to keep constant.
