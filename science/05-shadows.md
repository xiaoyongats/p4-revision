# Chapter 5: Shadows

## Seen in real papers
- Torch moved in an arc A → B (overhead) → C over a ball; graph of shadow length: **high → low → high** (ACS).
- Walking past a lamp post from A → B (under the lamp) → C: **V-shaped** graph (Henry Park).
- Wooden block at X (near torch), Y, Z (near screen): shadow **largest at X, smallest at Z** (CHS).
- Rubber ring moved closer to the screen: shadow **smaller**; to make it bigger **move the torch closer** to the ring (ACS).
- Ring changed to another material → **lighter** shadow because more light passes through (ACS).
- Object made of **metal, clear glass, cardboard, tissue** rings: predict the shadow pattern (CHS).
- **Cube in front of a ball** of the same size: shadow is a **square** (CHS).
- Cone: shadows possible = **triangle, circle**; NOT a **square** (Henry Park).
- Sheets X, Y (square hole), Z (circle hole): **which lets most / least light through** (Henry Park).
- Explain how a shadow is formed; aim + variables of a shadow-length experiment (CHS, Henry Park).

## 5.1 How light travels
- **Light travels in straight lines.** It cannot bend around an object.

## 5.2 Transparency (see also [04-light.md](04-light.md))
| Material | Light through | Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** (clear glass, clear plastic) | most | **none** (or very faint) |
| **Translucent** (tissue paper, frosted glass, tracing paper) | some | **light** shadow |
| **Opaque** (metal, cardboard, wood, rubber) | none | **dark** shadow |

## 5.3 Shadows

### How a shadow is formed (memorise, 2 marks)
> **Light travels in straight lines.** When an **opaque (or translucent) object blocks the path of light**, a **shadow** is formed **behind the object**, on the side opposite the light source.

- Needed: a **light source**, an **object** that blocks light, and a **surface/screen**.
- The shadow shows only the **outline**. It does not show colour or details.
- A **hole** in an object lets light through, so there is a **bright patch** in the shadow.

### Size of shadow (object between torch and screen)
| Change | Shadow |
|---|---|
| Object moved **closer to the light source** (away from the screen) | **Bigger** |
| Object moved **closer to the screen** | **Smaller** (closer to the real size), sharper |
| **Light source moved closer** to the object | **Bigger** |
| Light source moved further away | Smaller |

### Length of shadow from an overhead light (Sun, lamp post, torch moving overhead)
- Light source **low** (morning/evening, or far from the lamp post) → **long** shadow.
- Light source **directly overhead** (noon, standing right under the lamp) → **shortest** shadow.
- The shadow is always on the side **opposite** the light source.
- Moving the torch A → B (overhead) → C, or walking A → B (under the lamp) → C: the graph is **long → shortest → long** (**V / U shape**).

### Shape of shadow
- Depends on the **shape** of the object and **which side faces the light**.
- **Cone**: side facing light → **triangle**; base or tip facing light → **circle**. It **cannot** form a square.
- **Cylinder**: rectangle or circle. **Cube**: square/rectangle. **Ball**: always a circle.

### Two objects in a line
- The object **closer to the torch** casts the **bigger** shadow.
- Cube (6 cm) near the torch and ball (6 cm) behind it → the cube's square shadow is larger and **covers** the ball's shadow → you see a **square**.

### Method: predicting a shadow pattern (layered object)
For each region, ask **"How much light passes through?"**:
- Metal / cardboard (opaque) → **dark**.
- Tissue / frosted glass (translucent) → **light** shadow.
- Clear glass (transparent) → **no shadow** (bright).
- Hole → **bright**.

Then match the pattern to the option, region by region from the outside in.

### Method: sheets in a row (torch → X → Y → Z → screen)
- The light must pass through **all** sheets. Where the screen is **bright**, light passed through **every** sheet (including the holes).
- If X were opaque, the whole screen would be dark → X lets **most** light through.
- The darkest region tells you which sheet lets the **least** light through.

## Practice questions

**Q1.** Explain how a shadow is formed. [2]
**A:** **Light travels in straight lines.** When an **opaque object blocks the light**, a shadow is formed **behind the object**.

**Q2 (MCQ).** A wooden block is placed at X (closest to the torch), Y, then Z (closest to the screen). Which shadow heights (cm) are most likely?
(1) 30, 18, 12 (2) 18, 18, 18 (3) 18, 12, 30 (4) 12, 18, 30
**A:** (1). Closer to the torch → bigger shadow.

**Q3.** Umar moved a rubber ring closer to the screen: 10 cm → 14 cm shadow, 5 cm → 11 cm, 3 cm → 8 cm.
(a) What happens to the shadow height as the distance between ring and screen decreases? (b) Without moving the ring, how could he make the shadow bigger? (c) A similar ring of another material gave a **lighter** shadow. Why?
**A:** (a) The shadow height **decreases**. (b) **Move the torch closer to the ring.** (c) The new material **allows more light to pass through** (it is translucent).

**Q4 (MCQ).** A torch is moved slowly in an arc from position A (low, left), over the top of a ball at B, to C (low, right). Which graph shows the length of the ball's shadow?
**A:** A **V / U-shaped** graph: long at A, **shortest at B** (overhead), long again at C.

**Q5 (MCQ).** At night Alan walks from A, past a lamp post at B, to C. How does his shadow length change?
**A:** **Long → shortest at B → long** (V-shape).

**Q6.** Cassandra put an object on a table and moved it further from a low torch: 10 cm → shadow 7.5 cm, 20 cm → 15.5 cm, 30 cm → 23.5 cm.
(a) What was she trying to find out? (b) Which variable did she change, and which did she keep the same?
**A:** (a) To find out how the **distance of the object from the light source** affects the **length of the shadow**. (b) Changed: **distance of object from light source**. Kept the same: **type of torch**. (The length of shadow is what is **measured**: tick neither box.)
> Note: in this set-up the shadow falls **on the table**, and the trend is the opposite of the screen set-up. **Always use the data given** rather than a memorised rule.

**Q7 (MCQ).** Which shadow can NOT be formed by a cone?
(1) triangle (2) circle (3) square (4) triangle pointing sideways
**A:** (3)

**Q8 (MCQ).** A 6 cm wooden cube is hung between a torch and a 6 cm rubber ball, with a screen behind. What shadow forms?
**A:** A **square**. The cube is closer to the torch, so its shadow is **bigger** and covers the ball's shadow.

**Q9 (MCQ).** An object is made of rings, from the outside in: **metal**, **clear glass**, **cardboard**, **tissue paper** in the centre. Which shadow forms?
**A:** Outer ring **dark** → **no shadow** ring → **dark** ring → **light** shadow in the centre.

**Q10.** Isaiah lined up sheet X, sheet Y (with a square hole) and sheet Z (with a circle hole) between a torch and a screen. The screen showed: a pale outer area, a dark rectangle, a lighter square inside it, and a bright circle in the middle.
(a) State how a shadow is formed. (b) Which material lets (i) the most light through? (ii) the least? (c) Which is best for sunglasses lenses? Why?
**A:** (a) A shadow is formed when **light is blocked by an object**. (b)(i) **X** (ii) **Y**. (c) **Z.** It **blocks some light** so his eyes are not hurt, but lets **some light through** so he can still see.

**Q11.** Why is a shadow formed by a clear glass sheet very faint or absent? [1]
**A:** Clear glass is **transparent**: **most light passes through** it.

**Q12.** At what time of day is your shadow shortest? Why? [1]
**A:** About **noon**, when the Sun is **directly overhead**.

**Q13.** Why does a shadow not show the colour of an object? [1]
**A:** A shadow is an area where **light is blocked**; it only shows the **outline** of the object.

## Common mistakes
- Saying the **shadow** blocks the light. The **object** blocks the light.
- Forgetting "**light travels in straight lines**" in the explanation.
- Mixing up the direction: closer to the **light** = **bigger**; closer to the **screen** = **smaller**.
- Ticking the **measured result** (length of shadow) as "changed" or "kept the same".
- Saying a transparent object makes a dark shadow.
