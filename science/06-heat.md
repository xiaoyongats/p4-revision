# Chapter 6: Heat

## Seen in real papers
- **Correct thermometer position** in hot water: bulb fully **in** the water, **not touching** the bottom or sides, held upright (CHS, Henry Park).
- 4 mugs at 80, 60, 40, 20 °C in a 25 °C kitchen: temperatures after 5 min (ACS).
- Mixing equal volumes of 80 °C and 30 °C water → about **55 °C** (Henry Park).
- Hot metal blocks dropped into beakers: which water rises most → **smallest amount of the coldest water** (Henry Park).
- Which set-up boils first → the one with **less water** (ACS).
- Hot sand around a glass vs a metal bottle (CHS).

## 6.1 Temperature

- **Temperature** measures **how hot or cold** something is. Unit: **°C**.
- Our **sense of touch is not reliable**. (Hand from hot water feels warm water as cold; hand from cold water feels it as hot.)

| Instrument | Range | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Laboratory thermometer** | about −10 °C to 110 °C | liquids and air in experiments |
| **Clinical thermometer** | about 35 °C to 42 °C | body temperature only |
| **Digital thermometer** | varies | quick number reading |
| **Temperature sensor + datalogger** | varies | records temperature **over time** automatically, more accurate |

- How a liquid thermometer works: the liquid **gains heat and expands** → rises; **loses heat and contracts** → falls.
- **Using a thermometer correctly:**
  1. Bulb **fully in** the liquid.
  2. Bulb **not touching** the bottom or sides of the container.
  3. Hold it **upright**; wait until the reading is **steady**.
  4. Read at **eye level** while the bulb is still in the liquid.
- Key temperatures: ice melts **0 °C**; water boils **100 °C**; body about **37 °C**; Singapore room about **25–32 °C**.

## 6.2 Heat and temperature

- **Heat is a form of energy.** Temperature is a **measure** of hotness, not heat itself.
- **Heat flows from a hotter object/place to a colder one until both reach the same temperature.**
- Gains heat → temperature **rises**. Loses heat → temperature **falls**. (Except during melting and boiling, see Ch 7.)
- **Objects left in a room end up at room temperature.** Hot things cool **down to** it; cold things warm **up to** it. They never go past room temperature.
- **Amount of heat depends on amount of substance**: a pail of 60 °C water has **more heat** than a cup of 60 °C water (same temperature).
- **More water needs more heat** to boil, so it takes **longer**.
- The **bigger the temperature difference**, the **faster** heat flows.

### Method: mixing or contact questions
1. Draw an arrow **hotter → colder**.
2. The final temperature is **between** the two starting temperatures (never higher than the hottest or lower than the coldest).
3. **Equal amounts** mixed → final temperature is about **halfway** (80 and 30 → about 55 °C).
4. More of one liquid → final temperature is **closer to that liquid's** temperature.

### Method: "which water rises the most?"
The **biggest temperature rise** happens with the **smallest amount** of water and the **largest temperature difference** (coldest water).

## Practice questions

**Q1 (MCQ).** Which shows the correct position of a thermometer measuring hot water?
**A:** Bulb **fully in the water**, **not touching** the base or sides, thermometer upright. Wrong options: bulb above the water, bulb touching the bottom, or thermometer slanted against the side.

**Q2.** Why can't a clinical thermometer measure boiling water? [1]
**A:** Its range is only about **35–42 °C**; boiling water is **100 °C**, above its range.

**Q3.** Why does the liquid in a thermometer rise in hot water? [1]
**A:** The liquid **gains heat** from the water and **expands**.

**Q4 (MCQ).** Four mugs of 300 ml water at 80, 60, 40 and 20 °C are left in a 25 °C kitchen. Most likely temperatures after 5 min?
(1) 20, 20, 20, 25 (2) 30, 30, 30, 30 (3) 50, 40, 30, 25 (4) 70, 40, 20, 20
**A:** (3). The hot mugs **lose heat** and cool **towards** 25 °C (not below it). The 20 °C mug **gains heat** and rises to 25 °C.

**Q5 (MCQ).** Equal volumes of water at 80 °C and 30 °C are poured into cup G. Likely temperature?
(1) 28 °C (2) 30 °C (3) 55 °C (4) 110 °C
**A:** (3)

**Q6 (MCQ).** Identical metal blocks at 80 °C are dropped into: A 100 ml at 80 °C; B 50 ml at 20 °C; C 100 ml at 20 °C; D 50 ml at 80 °C. Which water has the largest temperature increase?
**A:** **B**: the smallest amount and the coldest water. (A and D: no heat flows because they are at the same temperature.)

**Q7.** Set-up A has a little water, set-up B a lot of water, same starting temperature and heat source. Which boils first? Explain. [1]
**A:** **A.** It has **less water**, so **less heat is needed** to raise it to boiling point.

**Q8.** A metal spoon at 80 °C is placed in water at 30 °C. Describe what happens. [2]
**A:** Heat flows **from the spoon to the water**. The spoon **loses heat** (temperature falls) and the water **gains heat** (temperature rises) until both are at the **same temperature**.

**Q9.** A bowl (1 litre) and a cup (200 ml) both hold water at 70 °C. Which has more heat? [1]
**A:** The bowl: it has **more water** at the same temperature.

**Q10.** Hot sand was put around a glass bottle with a thermometer in the air inside. The air temperature rose from 30 °C to 40 °C.
(a) Explain the rise. (b) With a **metal** bottle the air reached only 33 °C. Suggest why. (c) What happens to the sand's temperature after 2 hours?
**A:** (a) **Heat flowed from the hot sand** through the bottle **to the air** inside, so the air **gained heat**. (b) Metal is a **good conductor of heat**, so **more heat was lost from the metal bottle to the surroundings**. (This is the school's answer.) (c) It **drops to room temperature** (e.g. 30 °C) as the sand **loses heat** to the surroundings.

**Q11.** A hot drink at 90 °C is left in a 28 °C room for 3 hours. Final temperature? [1]
**A:** **28 °C** (room temperature).

**Q12.** Why does a hot drink cool quickly at first, then more slowly? [1]
**A:** At first the **temperature difference** with the surroundings is large, so heat is lost quickly; as the difference gets smaller, heat is lost more slowly.

## Common mistakes
- "The cold flows into my hand." Correct: **heat flows out of my hand**.
- Saying a cooling drink falls **below** room temperature.
- Saying more water has a **higher temperature**. It has **more heat** at the same temperature.
- Mixing 80 °C and 30 °C water and adding them up (110 °C).
- Thermometer bulb touching the bottom of the beaker.
