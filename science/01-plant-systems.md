# Chapter 1: Plant Systems

## Seen in real papers
- Label a plant diagram: which part **supports** the plant / **takes in water** (ACS, CHS).
- Direction of movement: roots → stem → leaves = **water** (Henry Park).
- Seedling: leaves make food; **roots grow before the shoot**; seed leaves **shrink** as their stored food is used up; seedling **dies when all leaves are cut** (ACS).
- 4 plants in jars with a **layer of oil**, roots in a **plastic bag**, fewer leaves: which set-ups to compare; what the aim is (Henry Park).
- "Part E (leaf) helps the plant make ___ in the light" → **food** (CHS).

---

## 1.1 Systems

- A **system** is made up of **parts that work together** to perform a function.
- If one part fails, the whole system may not work properly.
- A plant is a system: leaves, stem, roots, flowers and fruits work together to keep it alive.

**Q:** A bicycle is a system. Explain why.
**A:** It has different parts (pedals, chain, wheels) that **work together** to perform a function: moving the bicycle.

---

## 1.2 Plant parts and their functions

| Part | Main function(s) |
|---|---|
| **Leaf** | **Makes food** for the plant (needs light, water, carbon dioxide). Also lets the plant **take in and give out gases** (true statement in ACS paper). |
| **Stem** | **Holds the plant upright** and holds leaves up towards light; **transports water and mineral salts** from roots to other parts; **transports food** from leaves to other parts. |
| **Roots** | **Absorb water and mineral salts** from the soil; **hold the plant firmly** in the ground (anchor). Some store food. |
| Flower | Develops into fruit (reproduction). |
| Fruit | Protects seeds; helps seeds be dispersed. |

- Water and mineral salts move **upwards only**: roots → stem → leaves.
- Food moves from the **leaves to all parts** (up and down).

### Method: "Which part is damaged?"
- Leaves wilt → not enough water reaches the leaves (roots or water-carrying tubes damaged).
- Plant falls over → stem / roots not supporting.
- Plant slowly dies after leaves are removed → no food is made.

---

## 1.3 Leaves

- Leaves **make food** using **light, water and carbon dioxide**; oxygen is given off.
- Leaves are green because they contain **chlorophyll**, which traps light. Only green parts can make food.
- Parts: **leaf blade**, **leaf stalk**, **veins** (carry water into the leaf and food out of it).
- Leaves are usually **flat and thin**, so they have a larger surface area to **trap more light**.
- Special leaves: cactus **spines** (reduce water loss, protect from animals; the green stem makes food), pitcher plant (traps insects), water lily (large, floats).
- **Iodine test for starch:** iodine turns from **brown to blue-black** where starch (food) is present.
  - Variegated leaf: only the **green** part turns blue-black.
  - Part of the leaf covered with black paper (no light): stays brown, so **light is needed** to make food.

---

## 1.4 Stem

- **Support**: holds the plant upright and its leaves up towards light.
- **Transport**: water & mineral salts **up**; food from leaves to **all** parts.
- Some stems **store food** (potato, sugar cane, ginger) or **make food** (green stems, e.g. cactus).
- Types: **woody** (hard, e.g. mango tree), **non-woody** (soft, green, e.g. balsam), **climbing** (twine around a support, or use tendrils, to reach **more light**), **creeping** (grow along the ground, e.g. sweet potato).
- **Coloured-water experiment** (celery/balsam in red water): red lines in the stem and leaves show that water travels **up the stem** through **water-carrying tubes**.
- **Ringing experiment** (outer ring of the stem removed): food-carrying tubes are removed. The part **above** the ring **swells** because food from the leaves collects there. Parts below get no food. Leaves do not wilt at first because the water-carrying tubes (inside) are still there.

---

## 1.5 Roots

- **Absorb water and mineral salts**; **hold the plant firmly** in the soil; some **store food**.
- **Root hairs** increase the surface area so that **more water** is absorbed.
- Types: **tap root** (one main root: balsam, mango), **fibrous roots** (many thin roots: grass, rice), **storage roots** (carrot, radish, sweet potato), **prop roots** (extra support: maize, mangrove), **breathing roots** (grow above mud to take in air: mangrove), **clinging roots** (money plant), **aerial roots** (orchid).
- ⚠️ Potato and ginger are **stems**. Carrot and sweet potato are **roots**.

---

## Experiment set-ups you must recognise (from Henry Park)

| Set-up feature | Why it is there |
|---|---|
| **Layer of oil** on the water | Stops water in the jar from **evaporating**, so any drop in water level is due to the **plant taking in water**. |
| Roots **wrapped in a plastic bag** | Roots **cannot touch the water**, so the plant cannot absorb water. |
| Different **number of leaves** | To find out how the number of leaves affects the amount of water taken in. |
| Plant kept in the **dark** | To find out if light is needed (for making food). |

## Practice questions

**Q1 (MCQ).** A substance moves in a plant in the direction roots → stem → leaves. What is it?
(1) soil (2) food (3) water (4) seed
**A:** (3)

**Q2 (MCQ).** Which part of a plant holds it upright?
(1) flower (2) leaf (3) stem (4) roots
**A:** (3). (The roots *anchor* it in the soil; the stem holds it *upright*.)

**Q3.** Four similar plants were put in identical jars of water. Jar A: roots in water, layer of oil on top. Jar B: roots in water, no oil. Jar C: roots wrapped in a plastic bag, layer of oil. Jar D: fewer leaves, roots in water, layer of oil.
(a) Which two set-ups show that roots absorb water? Give a reason. [2]
(b) Why is a layer of oil added? [1]
(c) John compared A and D. What was he trying to find out? [1]
(d) State another function of roots. [1]
**A:**
(a) **A and C.** The only difference is whether the **roots are in contact with the water**.
(b) To **prevent the water from evaporating**, so the drop in water level is only due to water taken in by the plant.
(c) To find out how the **number of leaves** affects the **amount of water taken in** by the plant.
(d) Roots **hold the plant firmly** in the soil.

**Q4.** A bean seedling was observed for a week. Mark each statement True or False.
(i) The leaves allow the seedling to take in and give out gases.
(ii) The shoot grew before the roots.
(iii) The roots absorb water for the seedling.
**A:** (i) True (ii) **False**: the root grows out first (iii) True

**Q5.** As the seedling grew, its seed leaves became smaller. Explain. [1]
**A:** The **food stored in the seed leaves was used up** by the seedling for growth.

**Q6.** The seedling died after all its leaves were cut off. Explain. [1]
**A:** Without leaves, the seedling **could not make food**, so it had no food to grow and stay alive.

**Q7.** Part of a leaf was covered with black paper for 3 days, then tested with iodine. Which part turned blue-black? Explain. [2]
**A:** Only the **uncovered part**. It received **light** and could **make food (starch)**. The covered part received no light and could not make food.

**Q8.** Why are most leaves flat and thin? [1]
**A:** To have a **larger surface area** to **trap more light** to make food.

**Q9.** A balsam plant was placed in red water. After 2 hours, red lines appeared in the leaves. Explain. [2]
**A:** The red water was **absorbed by the roots** and **transported up the stem** through the **water-carrying tubes** to the leaves.

**Q10.** A ring of the outer layer of a tree trunk was removed. After some weeks, the part just above the ring swelled. Explain. [2]
**A:** The **food-carrying tubes** in the outer layer were removed. **Food made in the leaves** could not move down past the ring, so it **collected above the ring** and caused swelling.

**Q11.** How does twining around a pole help a plant with a weak stem? [1]
**A:** It can grow **upwards to get more light** to make food.

**Q12 (MCQ).** Which stores food in its roots?
(1) potato (2) carrot (3) sugar cane (4) ginger
**A:** (2)

**Q13.** Mangroves grow in soft mud. How do prop roots and breathing roots help? [2]
**A:** Prop roots give **extra support** to hold the plant firmly in the soft mud. Breathing roots grow **above** the mud and water to **take in air**.

**Q14.** A plant was transplanted and many root hairs were damaged. Its leaves wilted. Explain. [2]
**A:** Root hairs **increase the surface area** for absorbing water. With fewer root hairs, **less water was absorbed**, so the leaves did not get enough water and wilted.

**Q15.** Plant C has a long, thin stem; plant D has a short, thick stem. Fill in: "The stem of plant C is ______ than the stem of plant D." [1]
**A:** **thinner** (or longer/taller). Compare using the word "**-er**" and describe what you see.

## Common mistakes
- Saying roots absorb **food**. Roots absorb **water and mineral salts**; leaves **make food**.
- Saying the **shoot** grows first in germination. The **root** comes out first.
- Forgetting why a **layer of oil** is used (to prevent evaporation).
- Calling the potato a root.
- Writing "the plant needs sunlight to grow" instead of "the leaves need **light** to **make food**".
