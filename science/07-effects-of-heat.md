## 7.1 Heat transfer
- Heat flows **from hotter to colder** until both are at the **same temperature**.
- **Every object in contact** with something hotter **gains heat**; with something colder, it **loses heat**.
- Ice in your hand: your hand **loses heat** to the ice (feels cold); the ice **gains heat** and melts.

### Method: "Which objects gain heat?"
1. Find the **hottest** thing (e.g. hot water). It **loses heat**.
2. Everything colder that it touches, directly or through another object, **gains heat**.
3. Example: hot water in a basin + a glass of ice cubes inside → hot water **loses**; **basin, glass and ice cubes gain** heat.

## 7.2 Sources of heat
- A source of heat **gives off heat**: **Sun** (main source), fire/candle flame, lit lamp, electrical appliances (iron, kettle, oven), **friction** (rubbing hands), our bodies.
- **NOT sources:** **woollen jacket** (it only **slows down heat loss** from the body), ice, a mirror.
- In experiments, the **boiling water** or the **burner** is the source of heat.

## 7.3 Good and poor conductors of heat

| Good conductors (heat flows through **quickly**) | Poor conductors / insulators (heat flows **slowly**) |
|---|---|
| **Metals**: copper, iron, steel, aluminium (incl. foil), silver | wood, plastic, rubber, glass, ceramic, paper, cloth, wool, cork, **styrofoam**, **air** |

- **Air is a poor conductor**, so things that **trap air** insulate well (wool, feathers, fur, styrofoam, layered clothes).
- A poor conductor keeps **hot things hot AND cold things cold**.
- Metal and wood at room temperature are at the **same temperature**, but metal **feels colder** because it **conducts heat away from the hand faster**.

| Use | Material | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Pot / frying-pan body | metal | **good conductor**: heat flows quickly to the food |
| Pot / pan **handle** | plastic / wood | **poor conductor**: heat flows slowly to the hand |
| Cooler box, lunch box | styrofoam / plastic | slows down heat **gained** (cold drinks) or **lost** (hot food) |
| Woollen sweater | wool (traps air) | slows down heat lost from the body |

### Method: wax / pea / bead on rods
- **Shortest time** for wax to melt (or pea to fall) → heat flowed through **fastest** → **best conductor**.
- **Longest time** → **poorest conductor** → best for a **handle**.
- Keep constant: **length and thickness of rods**, **amount of wax**, **distance of wax from the hot water**, **temperature of the water**, **same starting time**. (Do NOT keep the material constant: it is the changed variable.)
- **Wax pieces along one metal rod**: the piece that falls **first** is **closest to the heat source**.

### Method: wrapped flasks / temperature–time graph
- **Steepest** drop (reaches room temperature fastest) → **best conductor** (e.g. **aluminium foil**).
- **Gentlest** drop → **poorest conductor** (e.g. **cloth**) → best for keeping **hot food hot** AND **cold drinks cold**.

## 7.4 Expansion and contraction
- Most things **expand** when they **gain heat** and **contract** when they **lose heat**.
- **Mass stays the same**; only the **volume** changes.
- Gases expand the **most**, solids the **least**.

| Example | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Dented ping-pong ball in hot water pops out | **Air inside gains heat and expands**, pushing out the dent |
| Stopper pops out of an empty bottle near a fire | **Air in the bottle gains heat and expands**, pushing out the stopper |
| Tight metal lid on a glass jar: run hot water over it | **Metal lid gains heat and expands more** than glass, so it loosens |
| **Gaps between tiles / rails / bridge sections** | Leave room to **expand on hot days**. On a hot day the tiles expand, so the **gaps become narrower** |
| Overhead cables sag on hot days | Cables **expand** and become longer |
| Balloon over a flask in hot water inflates | Air in the flask **gains heat and expands** |
| Same flask moved to ice water | Air **loses heat and contracts**; the balloon goes limp / is sucked in |
| **Two stuck glasses** (P inside Q) | **Ice cubes in inner glass P**: P **loses heat and contracts**. **Hot water around outer glass Q**: Q **gains heat and expands**. They separate easily. |

### Method (3 steps)
1. **What** gains/loses heat (*the air in the bottle*).
2. **Gains heat → expands** / **loses heat → contracts**.
3. **Result** (*pushes the stopper out*).

## 7.5 Changes in states of matter

```
          MELTING (gains heat)             BOILING / EVAPORATION (gains heat)
  SOLID  ─────────────────────►  LIQUID  ─────────────────────────────────►  GAS
         ◄─────────────────────          ◄─────────────────────────────────
          FREEZING (loses heat)               CONDENSATION (loses heat)
```

| Process | Change | Heat | Water example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Melting | solid → liquid | gains | 0 °C |
| Freezing | liquid → solid | loses | 0 °C |
| Boiling | liquid → gas, fast, bubbles throughout | gains | 100 °C |
| Evaporation | liquid → gas, slow, at the surface | gains | **any** temperature |
| Condensation | gas → liquid | loses | water vapour touches a **cooler surface** |

- During melting and boiling, the **temperature stays constant** (flat part of the graph).
- Other substances change state too: **liquid wax loses heat and freezes** into solid wax; chocolate/butter/ice cream melt by gaining heat.
- **Water vapour is invisible.** The white mist above a kettle is tiny **water droplets** formed when water vapour **condenses**.
- **Faster evaporation:** higher temperature, wind, larger exposed surface area, drier air.
- Evaporation of sweat **takes heat from our skin**, so we feel cooler.
- **Which side do droplets form on?** On the side where **warm air with water vapour** touches the **cooler surface**: outside of a cold glass; outside of an air-con car window; room side of a bathroom mirror after a hot shower.

### 3-step answer: "Where did the droplets come from?"
1. **Water vapour in the surrounding warmer air**...
2. ...touched the **cooler surface** of ___...
3. ...**lost heat** and **condensed** into water droplets.

## Practice questions

**Q1 (MCQ).** Which is NOT a source of heat?
(1) the Sun (2) a lighted lamp (3) a candle flame (4) a woollen jacket
**A:** (4)

**Q2 (MCQ).** A metal spoon in hot coffee becomes hot. Why?
(1) The cup loses heat to the coffee. (2) The spoon loses heat to the coffee. (3) The coffee gains heat from the spoon. (4) The spoon gains heat from the coffee.
**A:** (4)

**Q3 (MCQ).** Why did the ice cream melt after being taken out of the freezer?
**A:** **The ice cream gained heat from the surrounding air.**

**Q4 (MCQ).** Alison eats ice cream from a cup. Which is correct?
**A:** Ice cream **gains** heat; the cup **loses** heat (to the ice cream); Alison's mouth **loses** heat (to the ice cream).

**Q5 (MCQ).** A glass of ice cubes stands in a basin of hot water. Which gain heat? A basin B glass C hot water D ice cubes
**A:** **A, B and D**. The hot water loses heat.

**Q6.** Wax on rods A, B and C placed in boiling water melted in 20 s, 45 s and 35 s.
(a) What is the source of heat? (b) Which rod is the best conductor? Explain. (c) State a variable to keep constant. [3]
**A:** (a) The **boiling water**. (b) **A.** Its wax melted in the **shortest time**, so heat flowed through A the **fastest**. (c) **Length / thickness of the rods**, **amount of wax**.

**Q7 (MCQ).** Blobs of wax dropped off rods X, Y and Z after 2, 5 and 3 minutes. Which are correct? A X conducts heat better than Y. B X conducts heat better than Z. C Y conducts heat better than Z.
**A:** **A and B only.**

**Q8.** Peas fell from rods P, Q, R and S after 30, 65, 100 and 80 s.
(a) Arrange from poorest to best conductor. (b) Tick the variables to keep the same: (i) material of the rod (ii) amount of wax (iii) distance between hot water and pea. (c) Which material for a frying-pan handle? Explain. [2]
**A:** (a) **R → S → Q → P**. (b) **(ii) and (iii)** only. (c) **R.** It is the **poorest conductor of heat** (the pea took the **longest time**, 100 s, to fall), so **heat flows slowly** from the pan to the hand.

**Q9 (MCQ).** Wax pieces K, L, M and N on a metal rod fell after 40, 28, 17 and 63 s. Where was the heat source? (A under K, B under L, C under M, D under N)
**A:** **C.** M fell **first**, so M was **closest** to the heat source.

**Q10.** Boiling water in flasks wrapped in cloth, plastic and aluminium foil cooled to 30 °C after about 40, 35 and 15 minutes.
(a) Which wrap is aluminium foil? (b) Which wrap should be used for (i) hot food (ii) a cold drink? (c) Explain (b)(i). [2]
**A:** (a) The line that dropped **fastest** (15 min). (b)(i) **cloth** (ii) **cloth**. (c) Cloth is the **poorest conductor of heat**, so it **slows down heat loss** from the hot food to the surroundings the most.

**Q11 (MCQ).** Which is the best conductor of heat?
(1) metal cup (2) paper cup (3) plastic cup (4) ceramic cup
**A:** (1)

**Q12 (MCQ).** What happens to the gaps between pavement tiles on a hot day?
(1) become larger (2) become narrower (3) expand more than the tiles (4) gain heat
**A:** (2). The **tiles gain heat and expand** into the gaps. (A gap is empty space; it cannot expand or gain heat.)

**Q13 (MCQ).** An empty stoppered glass bottle is put near a fire and the stopper pops out because the ___.
**A:** **air in the bottle gains heat and expands**.

**Q14.** Glass P is stuck inside glass Q. Where should ice cubes and hot water go? Explain. [4]
**A:** **Ice cubes in glass P** (inner): P **loses heat and contracts**. **Hot water in the basin around Q** (outer): Q **gains heat and expands**. The inner glass becomes smaller and the outer glass bigger, so they separate.

**Q15.** A dented ping-pong ball is put in hot water and the dent disappears. Explain. [2]
**A:** The **air inside gains heat** from the hot water and **expands**, pushing the dent out.

**Q16.** Liquid wax in a tealight became solid 2 hours after the flame was put out. Explain. [1]
**A:** The liquid wax **lost heat to the surrounding air** and **froze** (solidified).

**Q17.** Frozen sausages had a thin layer of ice on the plastic. (a) State of ice? (b) After a while there was water instead of ice. Explain. [2]
**A:** (a) **Solid.** (b) The ice **gained heat from the surrounding air** and **melted** into water.

**Q18.** Water droplets formed on the outside of a glass of ice water. Explain. [3]
**A:** **Water vapour in the surrounding air** touched the **cooler outer surface** of the glass, **lost heat** and **condensed** into water droplets.

**Q19.** Ice at −10 °C was heated; the temperature stayed at 0 °C from minute 4 to minute 9. Explain. [2]
**A:** The ice was **melting**. The heat gained was used to change ice into water, so the temperature **stayed at 0 °C** until all the ice had melted.

**Q20.** Two identical wet towels: A spread out, B folded. Which dries faster? Why? [1]
**A:** **A.** It has a **larger exposed surface area**, so water **evaporates faster**.

**Q21.** In an air-conditioned car on a hot day, droplets formed on the windscreen. Which side? Explain. [2]
**A:** The **outside**. Water vapour in the **warmer outside air** touched the **cooler windscreen**, **lost heat** and **condensed**.

**Q22.** In a cold room, a metal chair leg feels colder than the plastic seat. Are they at different temperatures? [1]
**A:** **No**, both are at room temperature. Metal is a **better conductor** and **conducts heat away from the hand faster**.

**Q23.** Why do birds fluff up their feathers on cold days? [2]
**A:** Fluffed feathers **trap more air**. Air is a **poor conductor of heat**, so it **slows down heat loss** from the body.

## Common mistakes
- Saying a **woollen jacket** is a source of heat, or that it "gives warmth". It **slows down heat loss**.
- Saying the **gaps** expand. The **tiles** expand and the gaps get **narrower**.
- In wax/pea experiments, choosing the **longest** time as the best conductor (it is the **shortest**).
- Choosing aluminium foil to keep things hot or cold (foil is a **good** conductor).
- Ticking the **material** as a variable to keep constant when it is the **changed** variable.
- Saying the stuck glasses need hot water **inside**. **Inner glass → cold; outer glass → hot.**
- "Steam is water vapour." The visible mist is **water droplets**.
- Saying the temperature rises during melting or boiling.
