1

What makes something living?

Needs

food, water and air

Grows

gets bigger and heavier over time

Responds

reacts to changes (a lizard drops its tail when touched)

Reproduces

makes young of its own kind

Dies

every living thing dies eventually

  • Bacteria are living things, even though they are too small to see.
  • A graph showing mass going up month by month means it is growing, so it is living.
2

The big groups

Plants

Flowering plants and non-flowering plants. All plants make their own food.

Animals

Mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, insects.

Fungi

Mushroom, bread mould, yeast.

Bacteria

Very tiny. Seen only with a microscope.

3

Animal groups

MammalsHair or fur. Most give birth to young alive and feed them milk. Breathe with lungs.
cat, tiger, zebra, bat, whale, dolphin
BirdsFeathers, beak, 2 legs. Lay hard-shelled eggs. Not all can fly.
chicken, ostrich, penguin
ReptilesDry, scaly skin. Lay eggs. Breathe with lungs.
turtle, crocodile, snake, lizard, chameleon
AmphibiansMoist skin. Live on land and in water. Lay eggs in water. Adults have lungs.
frog, toad
FishScales and fins. Breathe with gills. Live in water.
guppy (gives birth to young alive), goldfish
Insects3 body parts, 6 legs, 1 pair of feelers.
beetle, ant, butterfly, cockroach, mosquito

Not all insects have wings

So "has wings" is not a feature of every insect.

Tricky ones

Whale, dolphin and bat are mammals. Frog is an amphibian, not a reptile. Spider (8 legs) is not an insect.

4

Plants

Flowering

Reproduce by seeds (from fruits).

  • papaya, banana
  • hibiscus, balsam
  • rice, grass

Non-flowering

Ferns and mosses reproduce by spores.

  • fern
  • bird's nest fern
  • moss

A papaya plant and a fern are alike because they both make their own food.

5

Fungi and bacteria

Fungi

Cannot make their own food. They feed on dead or decaying things, or food like bread. Reproduce by spores.

Bacteria

Living, very tiny. Some are useful (making yoghurt). Some cause disease.

Mould on bread grows faster when it is warmer

Mould grows best in warm, damp places. A fridge slows it down.

6

Reading flowcharts and charts

Heading for a groupFind what all members share. Cat + zebra → mammals. Mushroom + mould → fungi.
Missing questionIt must be true for the Yes animal and false for the No animal. Chameleon vs fish: "Does it live on land?" works. "Does it have scales?" does not (both have scales).
SimilarityFind a box where both went the same way.
DifferenceFind where they split. Say it for both: "S gives birth to young alive but T does not."
"Based only on the information"Use only the chart. Do not add your own knowledge.
"Is it definitely an insect?"Lays eggs + has legs is not enough. You need to know it has 6 legs and 3 body parts.
7

Watch out!

✗Bacteria are non-living.
✓Bacteria are living.
✗Mushrooms are plants.
✓Mushrooms are fungi. They cannot make their own food.
✗All insects have wings.
✓Some insects have no wings.
✗A whale is a fish. A frog is a reptile.
✓Whale = mammal. Frog = amphibian.

Remember in 3 lines

Living things need food, water, air · grow · respond · reproduce · die.
Mammal = hair · Bird = feathers · Reptile = dry scales · Amphibian = moist skin · Fish = gills · Insect = 3 parts, 6 legs.
Plants make food. Fungi cannot. Ferns use spores; flowering plants use seeds.
