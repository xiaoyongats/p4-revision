1

Magnetic or not?

Magnetic ✓

  • iron
  • steel
  • nickel
  • cobalt

Not magnetic ✗

  • wood, plastic, glass, rubber, paper
  • aluminium, copper, brass, gold, silver

Not all metals are magnetic. Aluminium cans and copper wire are not attracted.

2

Poles: attract and repel

Strongest at the poles

Most paper clips stick to the two ends.

Points North–South

A magnet hanging or floating freely stops with N pointing north.

Works through things

Magnetism acts through paper, plastic, glass and water, without touching.

Floating magnets

A ring magnet floats above another because like poles face each other and repel. A maglev train floats the same way.

Floating train bars (AB above CD)

A must be the same pole as C, and B the same as D. N–S over N–S ✓. S–N over S–N ✓. N–S over S–N ✗ (they attract).

3

Is it a magnet?

AttractedCould be a magnetic material OR a magnet. You cannot be sure.
RepelledMust be a magnet. Repelling is the only sure test.
No movementNon-magnetic material.
Tested from much further awayNot possible to tell. It was not a fair test.

Which magnet is stronger?

It attracts more paper clips, or attracts a nail from a greater distance.

4

Compass

  • The needle is a tiny magnet. Its N end points to the north.
  • Near a magnet, the needle's N end points to the magnet's S pole (unlike poles attract).
5

Making a magnet

Stroke method

Stroke with one pole, one direction, many times. The end where the stroke finishes becomes the opposite pole.

Electrical method

Coil wire around an iron or steel bar and connect it to a battery. This makes an electromagnet.

Iron

Temporary magnet

Steel

Permanent magnet

Magnets lose magnetism if dropped, hammered or heated.

6

Electromagnets

Stronger with

more batteries
more coils of wire

Weaker with

fewer batteries
fewer coils

  • Battery removed → no current → the iron loses its magnetism → it attracts nothing.
  • The core must be iron (magnetic). A wooden core does not work.
  • It attracts only magnetic things: paper clips yes, a wooden toothpick no.
  • To attract more pins without changing batteries or coils: use lighter / smaller pins.
  • Uses: scrapyard cranes (switch off to drop the metal), electric bells, maglev trains.

Picking set-ups for a fair test

A2 batteries · 20 coils
B3 batteries · 20 coils
C2 batteries · 10 coils

Testing batteries? Pick A and B (same coils). Testing coils? Pick A and C (same batteries). Only one thing may be different.

7

Watch out!

✗All metals are magnetic.
✓Only iron, steel, nickel, cobalt.
✗It was attracted, so it is a magnet.
✓Only repelling proves it is a magnet.
✗Picking set-ups where two things change.
✓Only the variable being tested may change.
✗An electromagnet works without a battery.
✓No current, no magnet.
✗Stroke with N → the end you finish at is N.
✓It becomes the opposite pole: S.

Unlike poles attract, like poles repel. Only repelling proves a magnet.
Electromagnet: more batteries + more coils = stronger. No battery = no magnet.
