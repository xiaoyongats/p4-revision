## 3.1 Matter

- **Matter** is anything that **has mass** and **occupies space**.
- **Mass** = amount of matter. Units **g, kg**. Measured with an **electronic balance / weighing scale / beam balance**.
- **Volume** = amount of space occupied. Units **cm³, ml, ℓ** (1 cm³ = 1 ml; 1 ℓ = 1000 ml).
- **NOT matter**: **light, heat, sound/music, shadows, electricity, feelings**.
- **Air is matter**: it has mass and occupies space.

## 3.2 States of matter

| Property | **Solid** | **Liquid** | **Gas** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Definite mass | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Definite volume | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ (fills the whole closed container) |
| Definite shape | ✅ | ❌ (takes the shape of the container) | ❌ |
| Can be compressed | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ |
| Examples | pebble, plastic bottle, cover, ice, sand | juice, milk, water, oil | air, water vapour |

⚠️ A **water bottle / plastic bottle** (the container itself) is a **solid**: it has a definite shape AND a definite volume.

## 3.3 Solids
- Definite shape and volume; cannot be compressed.
- Changing the **shape** of a solid (plasticine star → ball) does **not** change its **mass or volume**.
- **Sand, sugar, salt, rice** are solids: **each grain** has a definite shape and volume.
- Sponge "squashes" because **air in its holes** is pushed out.

## 3.4 Liquids
- Definite volume, no definite shape; flow; cannot be compressed.
- 200 ml of water in a beaker, round flask or conical flask: **still 200 ml**, different shape.

## 3.5 Gases
- No definite shape, no definite volume: a gas **fills the whole of its closed container**.
- Gases **can be compressed**.
- ⭐ **Volume of gas = volume of the container.** Pumping more air into a 1000 cm³ bottle: the volume of air is still **1000 cm³**; the **mass increases**.
- Pumping air into a **deflated** bouncy castle: the volume of air **increases until the castle is full (500 cm³)**, then **stays at 500 cm³**.
- **Air has mass**: an inflated ball is heavier than the same ball deflated (balance tilts down on the inflated side).

### Air-occupies-space experiments
| Set-up | Observation | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue in an inverted cup pushed straight down into water | Tissue stays **dry** | **Air in the cup occupies space** and **cannot escape**, so water cannot enter. |
| Same cup **tilted** or with a **hole in its base** | Water **enters**, tissue wet | **Air escapes** (bubbles / through the hole), so **water takes up the space**. |
| Blow air into an inverted cup with a straw | Water level **in the cup drops** | The **air** blown in **occupies the space** and **pushes the water out**. |
| Funnel sealed into a flask with plasticine | Water stays in the funnel | **Air in the flask occupies the space** and cannot escape. |

## 3.6 Measuring mass and volume

- **Mass of a liquid** = (mass of container + liquid) − (mass of empty container).
- **Measuring cylinder**: place on a flat surface, eyes **level** with the **bottom of the curved surface**. **Work out the value of each small mark** first, e.g. 10 ml between big lines with 5 small spaces → each small mark = 2 ml.
- **Volume of an irregular solid** (displacement) = **final level − initial level**. The object must **sink** fully and **not absorb water**.
- Several identical objects: divide the rise by the number of objects.
- **Volume of gas** = volume of the container.
- **Why 100 marbles of 1 cm³ won't fit into 100 cm³**: there are **air spaces between the marbles** because each marble has a **definite (round) shape** and cannot fit together without gaps.

### Method: ordering masses from balance pictures
1. For each balance, the side that is **lower** is **heavier**. Write "A > B".
2. Chain the facts: A > B, B > D, D > C → **A > B > D > C**.
3. Choose the matching option.

### Method: mass AND volume in one question
- **Mass** → read it from the **balance** (lower side = heavier).
- **Volume** → read it from the **rise in water level**.
- Work each one out **separately**. Do not assume that the bigger object is the heavier one.

## Practice questions

**Q1 (MCQ).** Which one is NOT matter?
(1) air (2) rice (3) water (4) shadow
**A:** (4)

**Q2 (MCQ).** Flowchart: Matter → "Does it have a definite shape?" Yes → A. No → "Can it be compressed?" Yes → B, No → C. Which is correct?
(1) A pencil, B air, C water (2) A pencil, B water, C air (3) A water, B pencil, C air (4) A water, B air, C pencil
**A:** (1)

**Q3.** Tick whether each has a definite shape and/or definite volume: (a) milk (b) plastic bottle (c) air [3]
**A:** (a) volume only (b) **both** (it is a solid) (c) **neither**

**Q4 (MCQ).** Samuel moulds the same plasticine from a star into a ball. Both have the same: A mass B shape C volume
(1) A only (2) C only (3) A and C only (4) A, B and C
**A:** (3)

**Q5 (MCQ).** 400 ml of water is poured from a beaker into a conical flask with no loss. What stays the same? A mass B shape C volume
**A:** A and C (mass and volume).

**Q6 (MCQ).** 300 cm³ of air is pumped into an airtight bottle of capacity 1000 cm³. Final volume of air in the bottle?
(1) 300 cm³ (2) 700 cm³ (3) 1000 cm³ (4) 1300 cm³
**A:** (3). The air **spreads to fill the whole bottle**; it is compressed.

**Q7.** A beach ball has a volume of 250 cm³ when fully inflated. Kumar pumps 20 cm³ more air into it so it feels firmer.
(a) What is the volume of air in the ball now? (b) Which property of gas explains this? (c) The beach ball is placed on a balance against an identical empty beach ball. Which side goes down?
**A:** (a) **250 cm³**: the air takes the volume of its container. (b) **Air can be compressed.** (c) The **inflated ball's** side goes down, because **air has mass**.
> Note: the Maris Stella answer key gives 270 cm³ for (a), but its own answer to (b), "air can be compressed", supports 250 cm³. Learn the rule "volume of gas = volume of its container". If your teacher marks it differently, ask them.

**Q8 (MCQ).** A sealed syringe has some water and some air. The plunger is pushed down slightly. What happens to the volumes?
(1) water decreases, air decreases (2) water same, air increases (3) water same, air decreases (4) water increases, air decreases
**A:** (3). Liquids cannot be compressed; gases can.

**Q9.** A measuring cylinder's large marks are 50, 60, 70. Between 50 and 60 there are 5 small spaces. The water is level with the 1st small mark above 50. What is the volume? [1]
**A:** Each small mark = 10 ÷ 5 = 2 ml → **52 ml** (include the unit).

**Q10.** Balance 1: B is lower than A. Balance 2: C is lower than B. Each block is then put into 50 ml of water: A → 80 ml, B → 59 ml, C → 75 ml. Which block has the largest mass, and which has the smallest volume?
**A:** Mass from the balances: C > B > A → **largest mass: C**. Volume from the water: A = 30, B = 9, C = 25 cm³ → **smallest volume: B**. (A has the biggest volume but the smallest mass. Bigger does not always mean heavier.)

**Q11.** 5 identical marbles raise water from 50 ml to 75 ml. Volume of one marble? [1]
**A:** (75 − 50) ÷ 5 = **5 cm³**

**Q12.** Why did the water in a vase overflow when more pebbles were added? [2]
**A:** **Pebbles are matter and occupy space.** The extra pebbles took up the space that the water occupied, so the water was pushed up and overflowed.

**Q13 (MCQ).** A crow drops rocks into a cup until the water rises to the top. This shows that solids ___.
(1) have mass (2) occupy space (3) have fixed shape (4) have no fixed volume
**A:** (2)

**Q14.** A tissue was stuffed into the base of a cup. The cup was turned upside down and pushed straight into a basin of water.
(a) Why did the tissue stay dry? [2]
(b) Holes were poked in the base of the cup. Describe and explain what happens. [2]
**A:** (a) The cup was filled with **air**, which **occupies space** and **could not escape**, so water could not enter. (b) **Air escapes through the holes**, so **water enters and rises** in the cup (to the same level as the basin) and the tissue becomes wet.

**Q15.** Zeus blew air through a straw into an inverted cup in water. The water level in the cup dropped. Explain. [2]
**A:** The air blown in **occupied space** inside the cup and **pushed the water out** of the cup.

**Q16.** A bag of chips has a mass of 150 g.
(a) Name the 2 states of matter in the bag. (b) Is the mass of the chips more than, equal to, or less than 150 g? (c) Explain.
**A:** (a) **Solid and gas.** (b) **Less than** 150 g. (c) The **air (gas) inside has mass** and the bag also has mass.

**Q17.** Jerome could not fit 100 marbles (1 cm³ each) into a 100 cm³ container. Explain. [2]
**A:** Marbles have a **definite shape**, so there are **air spaces between the marbles**, which take up part of the container's space.

**Q18 (MCQ).** Four balances show: A down vs B; D down vs C; B down vs C; B down vs D. Order from greatest to least mass?
**A:** A > B > D > C

**Q19.** An empty bottle has a mass of 200 g. Full of oil it has a mass of 380 g. Mass of oil? [1]
**A:** 180 g

**Q20.** Why should a sponge not be measured by the displacement method? [1]
**A:** It **absorbs water**, so the rise in water level is **less** than its actual volume.

## Common mistakes
- Saying a **container** (bottle) has no definite shape. The container is a **solid**.
- Saying pumping in more air makes the **volume** of air bigger than the container. The **mass** increases; the volume = container volume.
- Writing a volume **without units**, or misreading small marks on the cylinder.
- Saying sand is a liquid.
- In the inverted-cup question, forgetting to say the air **cannot escape**.
- Saying light, heat or a shadow is matter.
