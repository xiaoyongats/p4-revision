1

The basic rule

One (singular)

He walks · She has · It is · Mr Tan reads · The dog does

Many (plural) / I / you

They walk · We have · The boys are · I read · You do

Strange but true: a singular subject takes a verb with -s, and a plural subject takes a verb without -s.

2

Two subjects joined by "and"

Joshua and his sister = 2 people → were excited.

Sarah and Jane = 2 people → were thrilled.

The two men usually have breakfast together.

3

Each, every, everyone (singular)

each, every, everyone, everybody, everything, someone, nobody, no one, anyone → singular verb.

  • Everyone shares their ideas eagerly.
  • Each of the boys has a book. (the subject is "each", not "boys")
  • Nobody was at home.
4

There is / There are

Look at the noun after the verb.

  • There is a cat on the wall. · There was a long queue.
  • There are many kinds of food sold here. · There were many people.
5

Tricky subjects

"One of the …"One of the girls is late. (subject = one)
"with / as well as / along with"The boy with his friends is here. (only "the boy" is the subject)
either … or / neither … norMatch the noun nearest the verb: Neither Tom nor his friends were there. Neither his friends nor Tom was there.
A pair of …A pair of shoes is on sale. (one pair)
Many / several / a few + nounMany residents enjoy eating there.
6

Grammar cloze method

1. Underline the subject→2. One or many?→3. Present or past? (look at the other verbs)→4. Choose
Mr Raju is a retired policeman. Every morning, he (walk / walks) to the coffeeshop. The two men usually (has / have) breakfast together. There (is / are) many kinds of food sold there.
7

Watch out!

✗Each of the boys have a book.
✓Each … has.
✗The boy with his friends are here.
✓The boy … is.
✗There is many people in the hall.
✓many people → There are.

Remember in 3 lines

One → verb + s (is, has, walks). Many → no s (are, have, walk).
A and B → plural. Each / everyone / one of → singular.
There is / are → look at the noun after it.
