Parts of a poster

Pet Adoption Day Calling all pet lovers! Here's your chance to adopt a pet! Give it a loving home because every pet deserves a second chance. Date: 6 December · Time: 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. · Venue: Daisy Pet Centre Points to note: Dogs need regular walks. Cats enjoy playing with toys. Rabbits love to be cuddled. Birds may make some noise. Free pet grooming bags for the first 100 pet owners. Visit www.carepetshelter.sg for more details. Sponsors: Happy Vets, Animal Helpers · Organiser: Care Pet Shelter
Titlewhat the event is about
Purposethe main reason (here: find homes for the animals)
When / wheredate, time, venue
Organiser vs sponsorOrganiser runs the event · Sponsor gives money or prizes
More informationwebsite, phone number, QR code
Small printconditions: "first 100", "while stocks last", "children below 12 must be accompanied"
2

Common question types

"The main purpose is …"Look at the title and the first big line, not a small detail.
"Which is NOT true?"Check each option against the poster. Watch for opposite words (love ↔ dislike).
"If he wants to know more, he can …"Website / phone / QR code.
"Who will get …?"Read the condition carefully: the first 100 pet owners (not visitors).
3

Method

1. Read the question first→2. Underline its key word→3. Find that word on the poster→4. Match the meaning, not just the words
4

Watch out!

✗The sponsor is the organiser.
✓They are labelled separately. Read the label.
✗Main purpose = giving out free bags.
✓That is an extra. The main purpose is in the title.

Remember in 3 lines

Every answer is on the poster.
Organiser runs it, sponsor supports it. Purpose = title.
NOT true → check every option. Read the small print.
