1

Reading aloud

PaceNot too fast. Short pause at a comma, longer pause at a full stop.
ExpressionVoice goes up for questions, sounds excited for "!" and changes for a character's speech.
ClaritySay every ending: walked, books, asked.
PreparationUse the preparation time to read it in your head and spot hard words.
2

Tricky sounds

-ed endings

/t/ walked, jumped · /d/ played, cleaned · /id/ wanted, needed

th

tongue between teeth: three (not "tree"), they (not "dey")

Final consonants

don't drop them: hand, last, asked

Long vs short

ship / sheep · full / fool · live / leave

3

The conversation

Give more than one sentence for every question. Use this order:

Answer (yes/no, I think…)→Reason (because…)→Example from my life→Feeling or what I learnt
Examiner: "Would you like to adopt a pet? Why?"
AnswerYes, I would love to adopt a cat.ReasonCats are gentle and do not need to be walked.ExampleMy aunt has a cat called Mochi, and it always curls up on my lap when I visit.FeelingI think having a pet would teach me to be responsible.

Look at the examiner, speak clearly, and it is fine to say "Let me think…" before answering.

4

Listening comprehension

  • Before it plays: read the questions and options; underline key words (when, how many, why).
  • First listen: get the main idea and mark likely answers.
  • Second listen: confirm. Watch for changes: "The bus leaves at 3, no, sorry, at 3.30."
  • Note numbers, times and names as you hear them.
5

Watch out!

✗Answering "Yes." and stopping.
✓Answer, reason, example, feeling.
✗Racing through the passage.
✓Steady pace with pauses at punctuation.
✗Picking the first number heard in listening.
✓Listen for corrections and changes.

Remember in 3 lines

Read aloud: steady pace, pause at punctuation, say every ending.
Conversation: answer, reason, example, feeling.
Listening: read questions first, listen twice, watch for changes.
